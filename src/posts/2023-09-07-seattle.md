For a special race, I flew to Seattle, WA.

## Getting to My Motel 6

Without planning in advance and spending very little time looking, I got the motel I deserved. At first I thought I was clever. After landing in SeaTac I need only to take the light rail south one stop to it's terminus. From there it should have been an easy one mile walk with a single right turn. As it would turn out, Military "Road" was treated locally as a highway, and had no sidewalk, forcing me to pull my roller back directly behind me lest it get run over by high speed traffic. The motel itself was, minimal. But, I would not spend much time there.

## Shake Out Run

I was in town for a race in 2 days. I arrived on Thursday and after stepping around glass and broken VHS cassettes (I don't know) to get to my humble accommodations, I ran a shake out run down a public path which lead to Puget Sound.

Just 1 block west of where the train dropped me off earlier, the path started and lead down a ravine, following a creek towards the sound for about 4 miles.

Besides the frequent low flying air traffic and a section that passed a odiferous sewage treatment plant, it was a pleasant and well occupied trail. I saw a few other joggers, dog walkers and bikers along the way.

At the sound I followed a long fishing peer into the Sound to make my turnaround. There might have been otters splashing just below the waves in the clear saltwater. A sign on the pier informed visitors on which crabs were legal to catch.

## Time to Be a Tourist

I woke up Friday morning relieved that nothing terrible happened at this motel and I slept well. My plan was to put on a backpack and take the light rail north to downtown Seattle and not worry about looking like a dorky tourist with a camera. I would see all the big things.

The "1 Line" as it's called was fast but would take over 30 minutes before I'd exit the car into one of the 4 downtown stops. Sitting with my full backpack beside me an facing the opposite side of the train car, I craned my neck around to see the city go by between each stop.

After descending into a final tunnel I stepped out onto the platform for the University Street station and walked to my first stop, a coffee shop to caffeinate, eat a sausage egg sandwich and catch up on my journal. It was early and Pikes Market wouldn't be open for a couple of hours yet.

It was a hotel lobby and not very interesting to sit at, so I left and tried to walk my way to the Pikes Market area. When I made it there it was barely 7 AM and very empty. I could see where everything would be, but I did not see boats being unloaded by fishermen in boots and raincoats like I envisioned. It was more like a closed shopping mall.

I walked past the big famous sign and found the glistening, grotesque gum walls. It was too dark for good photos, but that was probably for the best. 

I followed a maze of alleys and closed shops to find the water front. Unfortunately, I'd timed me visit during a massive construction project the rendered most of Seattle waterfront behind Pikes Market a wasteland of closed sidewalks, traffic barrels and machinery. Though chain linked fences I saw the big ferris wheel sitting idly, still under a shadow as 8 AM passed. A fog gloomed off the bay and draped over the sky scrappers.

I occurred to me that I needed to get to the Space Needle, of course. Then I could come back here when everything was open. I pre-purchased my tickets sitting at another coffee shop and walked several blocks over to the Seattle Center. The line was already long but moved quickly. The Space Needle was full of thousands of other space needles, in that there were models of it everywhere. The Space Needle in varying states of construction, the Space Needle made out of Legos and of course the many Space Needles for sale in the gift shop. I was set on getting a Space Needle snow globe.

The line of visitors was broken into chunks and feed into an elevator which sped ear-poppingly fast up the tower with windows facing the city. I arrived at the top and started wandering around with my camera, dodging selfie sticks ducking past photos in progress.

The fog had mostly lifted and staff did a good job of keeping the observation deck from become too crowded. After a couple of laps I took a staircase down to a lower deck with a rotating, glass floor.

Have absorbed the experience to my satisfaction I queued up for the ride back down to find my snow globe. A recorded narration in the elevator informed me about the buildings I was looking at. The door opened into the round expanse of the Space Needle gift shop. With the assistance of a cheery gender fluid employee at the counter I choose a globe that made the most sense to me. Not too big, but not to slight looking either.
